Fills a gap no plan owned: every plan so far counts what the map *has* — plan 44 counts failed searches, plan 46 bands what is published, plan 29 picks the next source — but nothing measures what the map *lacks*, or shows a reader where it is thin. A map with no coverage view silently inherits its sources' biases and calls the result completeness. This plan is the honest-empty-state half of the product: where we are thin, how thin, and what fills it next.

## Why this plan exists

The map's first source is Wikidata, and Wikidata's coverage is measurably uneven — which means the pilot's published set will be uneven in exactly the same places unless something measures it:

- **The bias is quantified, and recent.** "Where the World went Missing: Geographic Inequality in Wikipedia and AI" (Research Square, posted Nov 12, 2026) analysed 21,270,978 geotagged records across 355 Wikipedia language editions: the Global North holds 275.6 records per 1,000 km² against 79.0 in the Global South, a density ratio of 3.49 (down from 5.60 in 2010, still substantial and stable over the past decade). Graham et al. reported about 84% of geotagged articles in Europe and North America in the mid-2010s. The map inherits this from its source on day one: a reader browsing West Africa will see a thin map, and without a coverage view neither the reader nor James will know whether that thinness is the map's ingestion lag or the source's own gap.
- **Gap measurement is a studied problem with a typology to borrow.** "Filling in the Blanks? A Systematic Review and Theoretical Conceptualisation for Measuring WikiData Content Gaps" (arXiv:2505.16383) proposes a typology of content gaps and classifies the measurement methods and metrics in the literature — the point this plan takes directly is that a gap is only measurable against a stated baseline: count your own rows and you measure volume, compare against an external reference and you measure coverage. The plan 26 pilot's own source queries are the natural baseline (see section 2): the pilot already asks Wikidata how many candidate events exist per class, so "thin" can mean "published share of what the source holds", not just "few rows".
- **The comparables that take coverage seriously say so out loud.** The World Historical Gazetteer gives "special attention to coverage of Africa, Latin America, and Southeast Asia" as a stated design goal, not a side effect — gap-awareness is part of the product, not a bug report. Its v3 index (47 million places, 67 million toponyms across six gazetteers) exists precisely because no single source covers the world evenly. The lesson for this map: the coverage view is where the multi-source strategy (plan 29) becomes visible to a reader, and where the next source is chosen with evidence instead of instinct.

What no plan owned is the measurement itself: the buckets (place, time, source), the baseline each bucket is judged against, the public page that admits the gaps, and the internal feed that turns gaps into the next source-expansion decision. Plan 44's weekly zero-result review already shows *demand* in thin areas; this plan adds *supply* — what the map holds there — so the two together say "readers ask here and we have nothing", which is the strongest possible source-prioritization signal.

## The coverage model: three views, one baseline rule

### 1. By place: H3 hexagons, density per area, never raw counts

- Published events are aggregated into Uber H3 hexagons (Apache 2.0, 16 resolutions, cell IDs as 64-bit integers / 15-digit hex strings, joinable across datasets on the ID alone). Resolution 2 (~87,000 km² per cell, country/region scale) is the world view; resolution 5 (~253 km², city scale) is the zoomed view. One resolution per view — mixing resolutions makes a colour scale dishonest, because a resolution-8 cell covers about seven times the area of a resolution-9 one and the counts are not comparable (kepler.gl H3 guidance).
- The cell value is **events per unit area** (or per land area where the cell is mostly water), never a raw count: a raw-count choropleth makes Russia and Canada look covered and the Low Countries look empty, which is the opposite of the truth. Hexagons are used instead of country choropleths for the map view because they do not flatter large countries and do not punish small ones; country bucketing (below) is the table view, not the map view.
- H3 cells are computed at publish time from the event's coordinates and stored on the row (one `h3_r2` and one `h3_r5` column), so the coverage query is a `GROUP BY` over published rows, not a live geospatial computation. The kepler.gl / deck.gl H3 layer pattern (a column of valid H3 indices becomes a hexagon layer with no geometry shipped) is the rendering model.
- Unlocated events (plan 36 abstentions still in the plan 34 review queue) are excluded from the place view and counted separately as "published but unplaced", so the place view never silently drops rows it cannot place.

### 2. By time: events per century, judged against the source's own counts

- The time view buckets published events by century (and by decade for the last 300 years, where volume supports it), using plan 32's strict start/end bounds — a fuzzy event contributes to every century its interval overlaps, weighted by overlap, never by its midpoint alone.
- The baseline for each bucket is the pilot's own source-query count for that class and century: the plan 26 pilot already runs per-class Wikidata queries, so the coverage ratio is `published / source-candidates` per bucket. A century at 90% published is covered; a century at 8% is a backlog, even if its absolute count looks large. Where no source query exists for a bucket (pre-1500, or a class the pilot has not whitelisted), the bucket is labelled "no baseline yet" rather than given a ratio — a missing baseline is itself a gap, and it is shown as one.
- The same ratio is computed per source (plan 29's source #3, Britannica link-only rows, and any later source), so the view answers "which source covers which era" as well as "which era is thin". A source that covers only the 19th century onward is visible as a shape, not discovered by accident.

### 3. By country: the table view, modern country, labelled as such

- The table view buckets by **modern country**, derived from the event's coordinates at publish time (plan 36 resolution, stored on the row). Modern-country bucketing is a deliberate choice with a documented caveat: the Wikidata community itself has not settled whether an event's country should be the country at the time or the country now (Wikidata RfC, "Current or contemporary location and country for events", Feb 2024) — P17 is used for both in practice, and there is no SPARQL feature that returns the historical country for a location at a point in time. The map therefore buckets by modern country, labels the table "by modern country", and gives the at-time view through plan 51's OHM boundary overlay on the map itself. If James wants at-time bucketing later, it is a second column computed from OHM, not a correction to this one.
- Each country row shows: published count, coverage ratio against the source baseline, plan 46 band mix (high / standard / minor), dominant source, latest publish date, and image coverage (share of events with a plan 48 image). The row is a link into the map filtered to that country and the current time window (plan 30 query), so a thin country is one click from being browsed.

## Two surfaces: a public page and an internal feed

- **Public `/coverage` page.** One page, three views (place hex map, time chart, country table), all computed from published rows only — never review-queue, tombstoned, or suggestion data (plan 37's never-export list is the never-show list here too). The page is honest by construction: empty cells say "no events published yet", never "nothing happened here". Absence on this map is a fact about the map, never a claim about history, and the page says that in one sentence at the top. The page is a normal indexed page (plan 38 sitemap, not noindex — unlike plan 55's embed route, coverage is content worth finding), and it links to plan 53's suggestion form with the thin area pre-filled as context ("suggest an event in West Africa, 1700s"), so the gap view directly feeds the suggestion flow.
- **Internal coverage feed.** The same aggregates, plus what the public page must not show: per-source queue depth (plan 34), plan 44 zero-result demand per region and era ("readers searched here and found nothing"), and the per-bucket publish rate over the last 30 days. This feed is the input to plan 29's source-expansion decisions and to the plan 44 weekly review: a bucket with high zero-result demand, low coverage ratio, and a flat publish rate is the next source or the next pilot-class whitelist change, in that order. The feed is a page in the admin surface, not a second public page.
- **Coverage travels with the snapshot.** Plan 54's monthly snapshot manifest (datapackage.json) gains a `coverage` block: per-century ratios, per-country ratios, and the baseline counts they were computed against, so a researcher downloading the data can see what it does and does not contain without visiting the site. The plan 59 public API gains `GET /v1/coverage` returning the same aggregates (same envelope, same caching), because a developer building on the map needs to know its thin spots before they query it.

## What this plan does not do

- No per-region quality score, and no ranking that reads as shaming: a thin region is a backlog, not a failing grade, and the page never orders countries by "worst first" as a leaderboard. The honest framing is the product decision; a score would turn it into a judgement.
- No automatic reprioritisation: the internal feed *informs* the next source-expansion decision (plan 29) and the next pilot whitelist change (plan 46); it does not make it. A human still decides, because a gap can be a source problem, a notability-threshold problem (plan 46), or a genuinely sparse record — and only a human reading the bucket can tell which.
- No new paid baseline data: baseline counts come from the pilot's own Wikidata queries and from WHG's public index counts where available. If a baseline would need a paid dataset, the bucket stays "no baseline yet" until a plan 29 source-expansion pass justifies it.
- No real-time coverage: aggregates recompute on publish (incremental, per affected buckets) and are cached under plan 43 like any other read-surface response. A coverage page that is an hour stale is fine; a coverage page that hammers Postgres on every visit is not.

## Sequencing

After the plan 26 pilot's first published set (coverage over the 44 curated events would measure the seed, not the product), after plans 30 (the query the views reuse), 32 (fuzzy bounds for the time view), 36 (modern country at publish), 43 (caching for the aggregates), 44 (the zero-result demand it pairs with) and 46 (bands in the country table). Before public launch: the launch announcement can point at `/coverage` ("here is what we have and what we are missing") instead of discovering after launch that the map's thin spots are invisible. Not a plan 26 pilot blocker — the pilot can publish without it, and the coverage view is the first thing that makes the pilot's unevenness legible.

## Done when

- `/coverage` renders the three views from published rows only, with H3 resolution 2 at world scale and resolution 5 when zoomed, density per unit area (never raw counts), and the "no events published yet" wording on every empty cell — verified by a test that loads a region with zero published events and asserts the page says the map has nothing there, not that nothing happened there.
- The time view shows per-century coverage ratios against the pilot's source-query baselines, with buckets lacking a baseline labelled "no baseline yet" rather than given a ratio.
- The country table buckets by modern country, labelled as such, with band mix, dominant source, latest publish date and image coverage per row, each row linking into the map filtered to that country.
- The internal feed pairs coverage ratios with plan 44 zero-result demand and plan 34 queue depth per bucket, and the plan 29 source-expansion decision for the next source cites it.
- Plan 54's snapshot manifest carries the coverage block, and `GET /v1/coverage` returns the same aggregates under plan 59's envelope and rate limits.
- No Splink anywhere in this plan (dedup remains Jev pairwise judging per the 2026-10-23 decision).
